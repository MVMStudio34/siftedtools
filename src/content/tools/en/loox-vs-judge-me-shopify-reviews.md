If you run a Shopify store and you're picking a reviews app, here's the short version: **Loox** is the reference when photo and video reviews drive your conversion, **Judge.me** is the budget choice thanks to its unlimited free plan, and **Yotpo** only makes sense at scale when you need reviews bundled with loyalty and SMS. All three are good. They're built for different stages and different categories of the same job. For the wider stack, see our [complete guide to AI tools for e-commerce](/en/best-ai-tools-ecommerce/). Shopify leans on third-party apps for reviews now, as its own <a href="https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/online-sales-channels/shop/product-reviews" rel="noopener">product reviews documentation</a> reflects, and WooCommerce stores hit the same gap: <a href="https://woocommerce.com/document/product-reviews/" rel="noopener">native WooCommerce reviews</a> cover text and stars but not the visual UGC these apps specialise in.

Most "Loox vs Judge.me vs Yotpo" pages hedge. This one commits. It's based on running all three on live stores, not paraphrasing their pricing pages.

## The verdict at a glance

The choice between Loox and Judge.me comes down to one question: is the aesthetic of your reviews a sales lever?

If you sell fashion, beauty, lifestyle or design, products bought partly with the eyes, Loox is the reference. Its photo carousels are the most polished on Shopify, visual UGC converts better than a text review, and the investment (from $9.99/mo) pays off from the first sales driven by visual social proof.

If you start on a tight budget or your products don't depend on visuals, electronics, technical accessories, consumables, Judge.me is unbeatable: unlimited free plan, SEO rich snippets included, solid quality for the price. No competitor plays at that free level.

In both cases, avoid Yotpo unless you're mid-market and want a complete suite, reviews plus loyalty plus SMS plus UGC. Powerful, but expensive and complex for a standard store.

## Loox: visual social proof done properly

*Our pick for visual brands.*

<a href="https://loox.io/" rel="noopener">Loox</a> made a clear bet: be the best at visual reviews, photo and video, and charge a premium for it. For its target segment, the bet pays off. If a customer photo is what closes the sale in your category, Loox is doing real conversion work that a text-first widget doesn't.

What it does well: the visual widgets are the most polished on the market out of the box. Photo carousels, mosaics, premium star badges, all tuned for conversion with zero CSS. Photo and video reviews are on every plan, not gated behind the top tier. Google Shopping ratings are native on paid plans. Its newer AI layer auto-sorts the strongest visual reviews first and translates reviews for international buyers, which is a quiet win for cross-border stores. There's a built-in referrals system on higher plans, so satisfied customers become a small acquisition channel inside the same tool. Because Loox lives on customer photos, the quality of your own product shots sets the visual bar those reviews sit against, our [best AI product photo tools](/en/best-ai-product-photo-tools/) guide covers the images that frame them.

The honest limit: there's no free plan (a roughly 14-day trial, then from $9.99/mo). Frame it as an investment rather than a cost, one that pays off when brand image matters and the conversion lift from visual proof outweighs the spend. Below a certain order volume, Loox Beginner can actually undercut Judge.me Awesome; above it, Judge.me pulls ahead on cost. So "Loox is expensive" is only half true, the crossover depends on your volume and how much your category sells on aesthetics.

**Best if:** you sell fashion, beauty, lifestyle, home or anything where the look of the review is part of the product, you want a premium feel without touching CSS, and visual social proof is core to how you convert.

## Judge.me: the budget choice par excellence

*The best value on Shopify.*

Judge.me is the app to reach for when the budget is tight and the reasoning is blunt: it does roughly 95% of what a premium reviews app does, at a fraction of the cost, and its free plan is a real offer rather than a marketing teaser. For a store that needs reviews to work and doesn't need a content shoot per review, that's the whole decision.

What it does well: the Forever Free plan covers unlimited review requests, photo reviews, and SEO rich snippets, the bit that puts star ratings into your Google results (Google's own <a href="https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/review-snippet" rel="noopener">review snippet documentation</a> spells out the markup behind them). That last point matters more than people credit. Rich snippets behind a paywall is how others nudge you onto paid tiers, and Judge.me just includes them, free. The Awesome plan at around fifteen dollars a month adds video reviews, Q&A, and cross-store sync, which is genuinely useful if you run more than one Shopify brand off one back office. The request-email builder is deep, with multi-step flows and the option to write raw HTML or switch Judge.me's sends off entirely and run everything through Klaviyo.

Worth knowing: the default widget is denser and more text-led than Loox's premium carousels, so if visual polish is your conversion lever, that's the trade-off, and Loox is built for exactly that. But for value per pound spent, no competitor plays at Judge.me's free level, and that's a real, verifiable advantage rather than a compromise.

**Best if:** you're bootstrapped, budget-conscious, starting out, or selling in a category, electronics, technical accessories, consumables, where the star count and the price matter more than the customer's photo.

## Yotpo: the suite, not the app

Yotpo plays a tier up. It's not really a reviews app anymore; it's a full e-commerce marketing suite, and its price and complexity put it out of reach for the average store. Judge it as a suite, because that's the only way it makes sense.

What it does well: the native Google Shopping and Google Seller Ratings integration is arguably the most complete on the market. The suite bundles reviews, loyalty, SMS marketing and UGC, so a brand that was juggling four or five separate tools can consolidate. The certified partner ecosystem is dense, which helps when you need implementation support rather than a help doc.

Where it falls short: the entry price around seventy-five dollars a month, scaling quickly from there, makes it overkill for the majority of stores under a thousand orders a month. The feature depth brings a real learning curve, some modules like SMS are geo-restricted, and the interface stays denser than Loox or Judge.me despite improvements. If you only need reviews, you're paying for a suite you won't use.

**Best if:** you're mid-market or enterprise, past roughly a thousand orders a month, and the return comes from consolidation, reviews plus loyalty plus SMS plus UGC, rather than reviews alone.

## Which one based on your situation

**You sell a visual brand (fashion, beauty, lifestyle, design, DTC).** Loox, first choice. The conversion you gain from polished photo and video widgets is exactly what the premium buys, and for products bought partly with the eyes it pays off from the first sales driven by visual proof.

**You're on a tight budget or just launching.** Judge.me free plan, no hesitation. It's genuinely unlimited with rich snippets included, so you get production-grade reviews at zero cost. Spend the money you saved on a bit of CSS. You can move to Loox later if visual proof becomes critical.

**Your products don't depend on visuals (electronics, technical accessories, consumables).** Judge.me is the rational pick. The star count and price do the work here, not the customer's photo, so stay on the free plan or Judge.me Awesome and pocket the difference.

**You have a high volume of text reviews.** Judge.me handles it cleanly and cheaply, and its request-email flows scale well without adding cost per review.

**You're mid-market (1,000+ orders) or multichannel.** Evaluate Yotpo seriously, but for the suite, not the reviews. The ROI is in centralising loyalty, SMS and UGC. If you don't need that, Loox or Judge.me still does the review job better per pound spent.

**You run several Shopify brands.** Judge.me Awesome with cross-store sync is hard to beat at fifteen dollars a brand. Loox doesn't handle multi-store as cleanly.

The classic trap cuts both ways. Buy Yotpo before you have a suite to fill and you're burning margin on idle modules. Cling to a free widget when visual proof is what your category sells on, and you're leaving conversion on the table. Match the app to your category and stage, not to a feature list.

## The verdict

There's no universal best Shopify reviews app, but there are two clear reference points. Choose **Loox** when the customer photo is part of the product and visual proof drives your conversion, fashion, beauty, lifestyle, design. Its carousels are the most polished on Shopify, and the investment pays off when brand image is a sales lever. Choose **Judge.me** when the budget is tight or your category doesn't live on visuals: the unlimited free plan is real, rich snippets come included, and no competitor plays at that free level. Reach for **Yotpo** only at scale, when reviews are one module in a loyalty-and-SMS suite you'll actually use. The only wrong moves are paying Yotpo prices for a reviews widget, or starving a visual brand on a text-first widget when its category sells on aesthetics. Pick on merit, match the app to your category, and don't buy ahead of where your store actually is.
